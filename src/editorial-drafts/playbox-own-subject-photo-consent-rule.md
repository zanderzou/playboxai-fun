**Playbox's specific eligibility wording is stricter than simply having access to a photo.** Its [terms](https://www.playbox.com/terms-and-conditions) require the uploader to be the depicted person. Read that requirement before assuming someone else's permission makes an upload eligible.

This article explains a published boundary. It does not create media, authorize an upload or provide a workaround for a service restriction.

## Three questions that should not be merged

First, who is depicted? Second, who controls the relevant photo rights? Third, what processing permission does the service receive? Those questions concern different relationships.

Owning a file on a device does not answer all three. A person might have received an image legitimately while still not meeting the provider's own-subject requirement. Conversely, appearing in a photo does not automatically establish every right needed for every use of the photograph.

## Read the specific clause before a general statement

A broad notice about consent should not be used to ignore a more specific eligibility condition. The terms identify an own-subject restriction. If another passage appears broader, ask the provider to clarify the applicable rule rather than assuming the broadest reading is permission.

For an illustrative non-sensitive example, a photographer holds a portrait of another adult and has a message approving ordinary publication. That does not by itself establish eligibility under a service rule requiring the uploader to be the subject. It also does not establish permission for every possible synthetic transformation.

## Processing is another scope

The [privacy policy](https://www.playbox.com/privacy-policy) describes handling of uploaded and generated content. Reading that policy helps explain what data categories are involved, but a privacy statement is not a substitute for meeting the terms' eligibility requirements.

Do not infer that a private processing claim grants permission to use another person's likeness. Likewise, a statement about content ownership should not be treated as an exemption from a service's own restrictions.

## Resolve uncertainty without another upload

If the eligibility wording is unclear for an existing situation, ask official support about the rule in general terms. You do not need to send an additional portrait or personal document merely to ask which clause applies.

Keep any response with the policy version and scope it actually names. A generated result appearing in an interface would not, by itself, prove that the upload was authorized or that every right was cleared.

The practical conclusion is narrow: the published own-subject rule should remain visible in any Playbox consent explanation. A general statement that someone agreed is not a complete substitute for that specific requirement.
